The AI commands. You drive.

A free real-time strategy game for Mac, Windows and Linux. Your AI commander runs the army; click any unit and play it like a shooter.

Get version 0.1.1 See how you play

Play it three ways

Drive any unit

Click one of your units and the camera dives in. WASD walks, the mouse aims, a click fires, R uses its ability, V switches first and third person, and Esc hands it back to the AI.

Command the AI

Press M for the command map and give your team's AI its orders: a stance, objectives (attack, defend, hold, man a Bastion), expansions and requests. It plans the rest.

Press the buttons

Click a building and press its buttons: train units, research, build. If it's a job the AI can do for you, the button queues it for your team's commander.

Drive it and it levels

The longer you drive a kind of unit in a game, the better every unit of that kind gets. Units the AI runs never level, which gives you a reason to drive the dull ones.

  1. Drive Only the units you drive yourself earn levels. Hand one back with Esc and it stops.
  2. Level Each level lifts every unit of that kind on your side, for the rest of the game.
  3. Keep one Every level offers two upgrades and you keep one: 216 across the twelve kinds, like "every Prospector pumps MH 20% faster".

How it's made

Concept to model

The model pipeline

Each unit starts as concept paintings. The one picked becomes a six-view turnaround, and the in-engine model is built part by part with Geometry Script and fitted against those views. The Atlas came to the game this way, and every snag along the road is written into the skill the agents follow.

The model pipeline skill

An engine-free core

C++20, tests in 7 seconds

The whole simulation, from the economy and pathing to combat and the AI commanders, is plain C++20 with no Unreal in it. Its tests build with clang and run in about 7 seconds. The Unreal layer draws the frames, plays the sounds and takes the input.

How the core is split

Made by AI agents

The skills are in the repo

Ringshadow is made with AI coding agents, and the skills they work from are published with the code: the buildings' light language, which tests a change needs, and the recorder that makes every clip on this page from the real game.
