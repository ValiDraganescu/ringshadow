# Mocap from a video model (step 8)

A walk (or any cycle a limb makes) measured from an AI video of the model itself, played back by the pose code from a table. First done on the Prospector, 2026-10-17; the tools are in `mocap/`, the files in `art/models/<unit>/mocap/`.

The idea, from the hobbyist write-ups of 2026 (a motion study of a dinosaur: a still, a marker suit painted on, a video model's walk, OpenCV tracks, a constrained rig): render the model with coloured dots at its joints, have an image-to-video model animate the still, track the dots in the clip, lift the 2D tracks to hinge angles, and hand the angles back to the rig. Human pose estimators are no use on a mech; the dots and the hinge math are the whole method.

## The steps

1. **Markers.** `mocap/<unit>-markers.json`: one entry per joint to track, the model point in the SceneKit frame (x ahead, y up, cells), taken from the catalog's part pivots and the pose code's pivot constants (the Prospector's: `legs_0` at (0, 0.56), the knee and ankle from `ProspectorShin`, the soles from `ProspectorSoles`). Name them for what the lift reads: `hip`, `knee`, `ankle`, `toe`, `heel`, then anything else worth watching (`body`, `drill`).
2. **Plate.** `uv run mocap/plate.py <unit> mocap/<unit>-markers.json` puts the RIGHT SIDE stencil panel (step 2's `stencil.png`) on a white 1280×720 plate, the unit in the left third with floor to walk into, one dot of a unique colour per marker (26 px, black rim), four black corner fiducials, and writes `mocap/plate.json` (each marker's pixel and colour, the pixels per cell). Look at `plate.png`: the dots on the joints, the guides and the panel border gone. A motion that acts on something gets the thing in the plate, so the video model knows what the unit is doing: `--prop <image> <x_cells> <height_cells>` puts a picture on white (an ore nodule from `grok_image`, `mocap/props/ore.jpg`) on the floor that far ahead; `--name <motion>` writes `plate-<motion>.png/.json` beside the walk's.
3. **Clip.** `mcp__orchestrator__grok_video` with `image` = `plate.png`, 720p, 6 s, `count` 2, `output_dir` `art/models/<unit>/mocap/grok/`, `name` `<motion>-v<N>`. The prompt locks the camera and names the motion: `art/models/prospector/mocap/prompt.txt` is the one that worked (orthographic side view kept, white background, the floor line, the gait in words, the dots stay on their parts, the corner squares never move, no shadows). Two clips cost about a cent; pick the cleaner from the three stills each returns.
4. **Track.** `uv run mocap/track.py mocap/grok/<clip>.mp4 mocap/plate.json mocap/track-<clip>` finds each colour's blobs per frame (HSV, hue ±7, saturation over 120), keeps up to two per leg marker (the far leg gets drawn with the same colours), follows them by continuity, and reads the fiducials. It prints the fiducial drift (under 0.05 px on both Prospector clips: the camera held) and how many blobs each marker had per frame, and writes `tracked-sheet.jpg` (every 12th frame with the tracked points). Look at the sheet: are the near leg's points on the near leg.
5. **Lift.** A walk: `uv run mocap/lift.py mocap/track-<clip> mocap/plate.json <clip>` builds the near leg's chain each frame (the knee, then the ankle blob nearest the shin's rest length, then the toe and heel beside it), takes hinge angles in the image plane (hip swing from the vertical, + forward; knee flexion, + with the shin trailing; the sole's pitch, + toe up), smooths them, finds the stride period from the hip's autocorrelation, sets phase 0 at the hip's upward zero crossing, and writes `gait.json` (32 bins) and `gait.png`: the measured dots over the game's current curves. Look at the chart: do the strides overlay (a steady gait), what the clip did that the game doesn't.
6. **Transfer.** Copy the table into `unreal/Content/Models/Gaits/<unit>_<motion>.json` (the prospector's files show the shape: named channels under `table`, radians or cells, `bins`; a clocked motion adds `period_s`). The pose function samples `FAcGaitTable::Find("<unit>_<motion>")` by phase in place of its sines: a walk by its stride phase (`PoseProspector`: hip and knee from the table, the legs half a stride apart, the knee clamped at 0, the ankle = the sole's pitch − hip + knee so the sole lands as filmed; the sink that keeps the lower sole on the ground stays); a clocked motion by `Time / PeriodS` with a per-unit offset (`prospector_drill`: `At("lean")` is the hull's roll, `At("push")` the drill arm's slide, `At("pitch")` the drill arm's SkZ, each unit a seventh of a stroke apart). `-AcNoGait` on any run plays the sines again.
7. **Check.** A profile pose sheet of one cycle, with and without the table: `art/models/prospector/mocap/sheet/sheets.json` (the walk: `heading` 0 for a profile; `stride` steps over one cycle, a cycle is 2π / 3.2 cells for the Prospector) and `sheet/drill.json` (the stroke: `mining` frames with `time` stepping over `period_s`, an `ore_0` `prop` under the drill), rendered by `-AcPoseSheet=<unit> -AcPoseSheetSpec=… -AcShot=…` in the model row (hidden run, 13 s), once with `-AcNoGait`. Save both under `mocap/sheet/`. Then `uv run unreal/Tools/hidden_run.py --out DIR --tests Autocraft.Gait`.
8. **Playground.** The motions moving, side by side: `sheet/live.json` is a `live` pose sheet (a walker at the game's 3.94 cells/s, a driller at its ore), and the ringshadow-video skill's `pose-sheet-prospector` and `pose-sheet-prospector-before` sims record it as 8 s clips (`uv run .claude/skills/ringshadow-video/scripts/record.py pose-sheet-prospector --out art/models/prospector/mocap/sheet/live`). Watch both before judging a table: a sheet hides timing.

## What the Prospector's run showed (2026-10-17)

- Two 6 s clips at 720p, 24 fps, 145 frames, from the clay stencil panel: the dots survived on every frame, the same size and hue; the corner fiducials moved under 0.05 px. The video model keeps a flat orthographic clay figure as one.
- It drew the far leg (hidden in the plate) and copied the marker set onto it, hue-shuffled: the far knee got cyan (the heel's colour) in one clip, yellow in the other. So the tracker keeps two blobs per leg colour and the lift picks the near leg by the chain, not by colour.
- The near leg's thigh stretched up to 45% of its rest length between frames, the shin 6%: limbs morph, so the lift uses angles only, never lengths.
- The knee bent backwards (−34°) while the leg trailed, in the frames too, not a tracking slip: the model does not respect a hinge's direction. The transfer clamps the knee at 0.
- Three strides overlay cleanly: period 2.0 s, a high-stepping gait with the hip at −53° to +61° and the knee up to 92° (the game's sines had ±26° and the knee near straight for half the stride), the sole flat through stance and toe-down at push-off.
- The stride in the clip is 0.44 cells per 2 s; the game keeps its own phase (a cycle per 1.96 cells), so the shapes transfer and the speed stays the game's.
- The second clip pitched the drill down late in the clip and lost the knee for 6 frames; the first was the one used.

## What the Prospector's drill showed (2026-10-18)

- The plate for a motion on something needs the thing: `plate-drill.png` has a clay ore nodule (`grok_image`, matte dark rock with opal veins on white) a cell and a half ahead, the drill tip at its face, and an eighth marker on the drill arm's pivot (`shoulder`, violet). Both clips drilled the rock, with sparks and chips, and kept the eight dots and the fiducials (drift under 0.05 px).
- The video model did not give the stroke the prompt asked for (a push a second). Clip 1 was one long dive over 6 s: the hull tilted 14°, the drill marker jumped to the hull for 10 frames and the drill pitched through 180° in the tracks (`arm_len_ratio` down to 0.3), the knee bent back 32°: unusable. Clip 2 was a slow rocking stroke of 3.75 s (a weak autocorrelation peak, 0.32): the hull's top back by 9° as the drill dips 17° into the rock, the arm back by 8 cm, the knee bent backwards by 28° (the model's joint direction again, so the legs were not transferred). That table is `Gaits/prospector_drill.json`.
- Three channels went into the game: `lean` (the hull about its root; the game's own lean was 6° to 8° forward), `push` (the game's was a 3 cm forward stroke at 2.4 rad/s) and `pitch` (new: the drill arm had no rotation). The sparks stay at the body's emitter, not on the dipping tip.
- A 6 s clip holds one or two strokes at most, and the model picks its own tempo: ask for more clips, or a 10 s one, and keep the one whose autocorrelation has a real peak.
